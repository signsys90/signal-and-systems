## Executive Summary

SaniCONNECT was a private collaboration platform I planned, built, launched, and administered for SaniGLAZE International. It gave the whole SaniGLAZE community (franchise members, technicians, sales members, and the corporate team and leadership) one private place to share before-and-after project photographs, exchange practical tips and field experience, work through technical questions, and talk shop, outside of public social networks. I built it on WordPress in about two months, customized and branded it for SaniGLAZE, hosted it on the company's server, and launched it in Spring 2024. It grew to more than 40 members, who reached it on desktop, tablet, and mobile devices and through companion apps on the Apple App Store and Google Play.

## Business Context

**Why a franchise organization benefits from its own community.** A franchise network depends on practical knowledge held by the people doing the work. Owners, technicians, and sales staff in different territories run into similar jobs, surfaces, materials, and customer situations, and what one location learns can help another. Corporate teams need a way to stay close to that experience and support it.

Public social networks such as LinkedIn are built for public, professional audiences. They are not the right setting for every internal business discussion, particularly technical troubleshooting, proprietary processes, internal operating practices, and franchise-specific knowledge.

**What SaniCONNECT addressed.** I identified the need for a dedicated environment where everyone in the SaniGLAZE organization could interact and share relevant information on a platform built for their business community. It was for internal communication and knowledge exchange, not a public-facing marketing website.

This page does not describe specific problems with earlier communication methods, adoption barriers, or costs, because none are documented.

## The Challenge

SaniGLAZE's franchise members, technicians, sales members, and corporate team needed an organization-specific place to:

- Share before-and-after photographs of completed projects.
- Exchange tips, tricks, and practical experience.
- Raise and discuss technical questions and troubleshooting.
- Share relevant operational knowledge.
- Take part in general internal conversation.

The requirement was a private community designed for this audience: one that reflected the SaniGLAZE identity, worked on the devices people use, and kept internal knowledge in an internal setting.

I chose to adapt an established community platform rather than build social software from scratch, then shaped it around SaniGLAZE.

## Technical and Strategic Considerations

These are general considerations for any internal franchise community, drawn from the kind of work SaniCONNECT involved. They are not a record of specific lessons documented during the project.

- **Participation is the product.** A private community only has value if people post. Seeded content, visible corporate participation, and an easy first post matter more than features.
- **Privacy is a promise to the members.** Internal discussion only works if members trust who can see their posts. Clear access rules, and clear guidance on what not to post, such as customer personal data, support that trust.
- **Knowledge has to be findable later.** Conversation feeds move fast. A good troubleshooting answer is most valuable when it is pinned, tagged, or copied into a reference area where the next person can find it.
- **Mobile access fits field work.** Technicians and sales members work away from a desk. Mobile and app access put the community where the work happens.
- **Customization creates upkeep.** Each custom page, plugin, code change, and app release adds maintenance. WordPress, plugins, and app listings all need updates, and someone has to own them.
- **Administration is ongoing.** Approving members, removing departed users, moderating content, and keeping the platform current are continuing responsibilities, not launch tasks.
