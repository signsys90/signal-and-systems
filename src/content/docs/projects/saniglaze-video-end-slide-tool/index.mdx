## Overview

The SaniGLAZE Video End-Slide Generator is a self-service web application designed to simplify the production of localized marketing videos for SaniGLAZE International franchise members.

The application replaces a manual, request-based video production workflow with a guided process that allows franchise members to select a corporate video, personalize its closing slide with their contact information, and generate a finished video ready for distribution.

Developed with assistance from OpenAI Codex, the application translates a recurring operational requirement into a repeatable software workflow. Its design combines a standardized brand template, configurable contact information, automated video composition, and a single downloadable output.

The result is a business system designed to make localized marketing content easier to produce without requiring franchise members to operate professional video-editing software.

## 1. The Business Problem

### A repeatable task trapped in a manual workflow

Corporate marketing videos provided franchise members with centrally produced promotional content. However, individual franchise locations needed to append their own contact information before using those videos in local marketing activities.

The existing process introduced a manual dependency: franchise members needed to request a localized version rather than generate one independently.

Each request represented another task for the central team, even when the underlying work was essentially identical.

The problem had three dimensions:

- **Operational inefficiency:** A repeatable task required manual intervention.
- **Limited scalability:** Production requests increased the workload on the central marketing function.
- **Brand governance:** Allowing each franchise to edit videos independently could introduce inconsistencies in branding and presentation.

The challenge was not simply to make video editing easier. It was to redesign the process so that franchise members could complete a common marketing task independently while preserving corporate brand standards.

### The opportunity

A standardized, self-service application could automate the repetitive work, reduce dependence on centralized production, and make localized video creation accessible to users without video-editing experience.

The goal was to turn a manual service request into a consistent, software-driven workflow.

## 2. Solution Design

The application was designed around a simple principle:

**Make the approved process the easiest process to follow.**

Rather than providing users with an unrestricted video editor, the application guides them through a small number of defined choices.

The common workflow uses approved corporate video assets, stored franchise information, and a predefined branded end-slide template. Additional upload options accommodate exceptions without requiring users to leave the standardized process.

### The user experience

**Step 1 — Select a video**

Users choose an approved corporate video from a centrally managed library or upload their own video when customization requires different footage.

**Step 2 — Select contact information**

Users choose available franchise contact information or provide their own details through manual entry or an upload workflow.

**Step 3 — Generate the end slide**

The application places the selected contact information into a standardized branded layout. Users specify how long the closing slide should remain visible.

**Step 4 — Process and download**

The application combines the source video with the generated closing segment and produces one finished video file for download.

The intended experience minimizes technical decisions and keeps the user focused on selecting the correct content rather than learning video-production tools.

### Two paths, one standardized output

The workflow supports two primary usage patterns.

| Capability | Quick-select workflow | Custom workflow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Video source | Approved corporate video library | User-uploaded video |
| Contact information | Select available franchise details | Enter or upload contact details |
| Branding | Standardized branded template | Same standardized branded template |
| Closing duration | User configurable | User configurable |
| Processing | Automated video composition | Automated video composition |
| Deliverable | Single finished video | Single finished video |

The quick-select workflow is optimized for routine requests. The custom workflow provides additional flexibility for franchise members with different content or contact-information requirements.

Both paths are designed to produce a consistent, ready-to-use deliverable.

## 3. Architecture and Technical Highlights

The application can be understood as five cooperating functions. They are described by role only; the original languages, frameworks, libraries, and hosting are not documented.

| Function | What it does in the workflow |
| --- | --- |
| User interface | Lets franchise members choose a video, supply or select contact information, set the closing duration, and retrieve the finished file |
| Workflow coordination | Checks that the required inputs are present and moves the request from selections to a finished video |
| End-slide generation | Places the contact information into a fixed, branded template, separating variable content from the approved design |
| Video composition | Combines the source footage and the generated closing segment into one playable video |
| File delivery | Makes the finished video available as a single download |

### Design highlights

- **Template, not canvas.** Users fill designated fields in a fixed branded layout rather than designing a slide, which keeps the output on brand.
- **Two inputs, one output.** The quick-select and custom paths differ only in where the video and contact details come from. Both end in the same standardized deliverable.
- **Processing off the user's computer.** Users make choices; the application does the composition work, so no one needs video-editing software.

## 9. Security, Privacy, and Brand Governance

The application processes video files and contact information, so a responsible implementation should consider data handling and brand integrity alongside usability.

### Brand governance

The branded template separates fixed corporate design elements from the fields users can personalize.

This design helps constrain variation while preserving the flexibility needed for local marketing.

### File handling

Uploaded files should be validated for permitted formats and reasonable size limits. Processing should occur in controlled locations, and temporary assets should be removed according to an established retention policy.

### Input validation

Contact information and filenames should be handled safely. User-supplied content should not be treated as trusted application instructions or executable content.

### Access control

Where the application contains stored franchise information or restricted corporate assets, access should be limited to authorized users.

## 10. Lessons Learned

<Callout type="lesson" title="Make the standard the easiest path">
When the approved workflow is also the fastest workflow, users have less reason to seek alternative processes.

The quick-select path makes routine video localization simpler while keeping the output aligned with the established brand template.
</Callout>

<Callout type="lesson" title="Automate the process, not just an individual task">
The opportunity extended beyond generating a closing graphic.

The application connects asset selection, contact-information handling, template rendering, media composition, and delivery into one workflow.

That end-to-end design is what makes the solution useful as a business system.
</Callout>

<Callout type="lesson" title="Preserve flexibility without sacrificing consistency">
Not every franchise member has identical content requirements.

Supporting custom video uploads and alternative contact-information inputs accommodates exceptions while keeping the output anchored to a common template.
</Callout>

<Callout type="lesson" title="Build around the user's actual job">
Franchise members needed a finished marketing video, not another professional editing tool to learn.

The guided workflow focuses on the outcome the user wants to achieve and abstracts the technical steps required to produce it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="lesson" title="Apply AI-assisted development to operational problems">
Codex provided a means of implementing an application around a defined business requirement.

The project demonstrates the potential of AI-assisted development to turn workflow knowledge into a practical software solution, with the business process and user requirements guiding the implementation.
