## Goal

Put a usable 2 m / 70 cm amateur radio station in the truck, with an external antenna for better range than the radio's own rubber antenna, without sharing power or grounds with the emergency lighting system. As a licensed operator (KR4JSQ) and a Duval County ESF-2 emergency communications volunteer, I want a radio in the truck that works when I need it.

## Constraints

- **Roof:** 2017 F-150 Lariat SuperCrew with a panoramic roof, so there's no roof mount. The antenna goes on a door-frame bracket instead.
- **Separation:** the radio must stay electrically separate from the Whelen CanTrol WC system. Whelen's own guide (14580G, p.1) says never to power warning equipment from the same circuit as radio equipment, or share its ground.
- **No drilling:** the NMO bracket clamps to the door frame, so no hole goes in the aluminum body.
- **Callsign:** KR4JSQ.

## Architecture

```text
BaoFeng BF-F8HP (console tray)
   └─ SMA jumper ─ PL-259 ─ Comet HD-5NMO coax
                                └─ NMO bracket (rear passenger door, top rear corner)
                                       └─ 7.5" dual-band 2 m / 70 cm antenna
Power: radio's own battery pack (nothing shared with the truck or the CanTrol)
```

## Wiring and configuration

The radio runs on its own battery pack, so it shares nothing with the truck's electrical system.

If it's ever powered from the truck (a battery eliminator or a mobile rig later), the rules are fixed:

- Its own fused feed from the battery, fused within 2 ft of the source.
- Its own ground to the Ford ground point or the side of a steel frame rail.
- Never the CanTrol's fuse block or grounds.
- Never the battery negative post. On a 2015+ F-150 all negative current has to pass through the battery monitoring sensor (Ford SVE Bulletin Q-231).

## Build steps

1. Mount the Comet HD-5NMO at the top rear corner of the rear passenger-side door.
2. Fit the antenna on the NMO base.
3. Route the coax to the console. Keep it clear of door hinges and pinch points, and away from lighting and siren wiring. Where it has to cross them, cross at right angles.
4. Connect the radio to the coax with a short SMA jumper.
5. Check the door still opens and closes fully without pinching the coax.

## Safety notes

- Keep the coax clear of door hinges and pinch points.
- After the warning system is installed, key the radio with the lights and siren running and listen for interference both ways: noise on the radio, and any glitch on the CanTrol.
- Don't transmit while someone is touching the antenna.

## Lessons learned

- A door-frame NMO bracket is a clean answer for a panoramic-roof truck. It needs no hole in the body, and it keeps the antenna high.
