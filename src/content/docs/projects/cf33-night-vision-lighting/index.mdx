**Night vision matters when you're on duty.**

A client running a Panasonic Toughbook CF-33 in a public safety vehicle came to us with a real problem. During overnight shifts, the factory backlit keyboard wasn't cutting it. It was either too bright for dark-adapted eyes or not readable enough to work quickly, and neither is acceptable in the field.

Signal & Systems Engineering designed a practical, cost-effective fix that went well beyond what the client expected. Every factory function stayed intact, and the installation footprint stayed minimal. Nothing permanent, nothing in the way, and no compromise on the tools responders depend on.

That's how we approach every build. Solve the real-world problem, respect the original equipment, and keep it simple enough to trust at 3 AM.

Then we took it a step further. We designed the package to also provide night-vision-safe ground lighting when the CF-33 is carried by its handle, so the responder can move from the vehicle to the scene without losing their dark adaptation or their footing.

## Goal

Give a responder working at night a keyboard they can read quickly without wrecking their dark adaptation, and keep that same night-safe light with them when they pick up the computer and walk to the scene.

"Done" means:

- The keys are readable at a glance in a dark cab, under red light.
- Every factory function of the CF-33 still works, including the factory keyboard backlight.
- Nothing on the Toughbook is drilled, cut or permanently changed, and the lighting does not get in the way of typing, docking or carrying.
- When the CF-33 is carried by its handle, the lighting falls on the ground ahead of the responder's feet.

## Why red light

Red light is the usual choice for night work because it disturbs dark adaptation much less than white or blue light, which is why it shows up in cockpits, on ships and in tactical flashlights. The goal here is the same: enough light to read the keys and see the ground, at a color that lets the eyes stay adjusted to the dark.

## Constraints

- **Respect the original equipment.** The CF-33 is a rugged, mission-critical computer. No drilling, cutting or permanent adhesive changes to the unit, and no loss of any factory function.
- **Minimal footprint.** The lighting must not block the keyboard, touchpad, display, ports, the detach latch or the carry handle.
- **Readable, not bright.** Too much light defeats the purpose. The red light has to be just enough to read keys and see footing.
- **Cost-effective.** The client asked for a practical fix, not a custom electronics project.
- **Simple enough to trust at 3 AM.** No software, no settings to manage, nothing that can confuse a tired operator mid-shift.

## What was built

From the photos of the finished unit, the package has two red LED light bars:

1. **Keyboard bar.** A slim red LED bar runs along the hinge line, at the bottom edge of the display, and casts red light down across the keys.
2. **Front-edge bar.** A second red LED bar, in a clear housing, runs along the front edge of the keyboard base, below the palm rest.

The CF-33's carry handle is on the opposite edge of the base. When the unit is closed and carried by that handle, the front-edge bar faces the ground, so the same bar that lights the desk in front of the keyboard becomes a downward ground light while walking.

## Safety notes

- **Keep it night-vision-safe.** Red only, and no brighter than needed. A light bright enough to dazzle the operator defeats the purpose.
- **Nothing in the way.** Keep cables and bars clear of the keyboard, ports, latch, handle and any vehicle dock or mount.
- **Vehicle power.** If the lighting is ever powered from the vehicle, it needs its own fuse at the source and cable routing clear of airbag deployment zones, like any other in-cab equipment.
- **No permanent changes.** The build is meant to come off without leaving damage, so the computer can go back to factory condition at any time.

## Lessons learned

- **Solve the actual problem.** The issue was not "the keyboard is too dark". It was that neither factory setting worked for dark-adapted eyes. A different color of light solved what more or less brightness could not.
- **Reuse the same hardware for a second job.** Placing the front bar where it lands on the ground in carry position turned a keyboard fix into a scene-walk light at no extra footprint.
