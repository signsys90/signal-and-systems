## Goal

Launch an online apparel brand for Jacksonville fan culture: Duval pride, 904 identity and game-day life in teal and black. It sells to local fans, Jacksonville natives who've moved away, and UK fans who follow the team's London games. The brand stays independent. It never uses NFL or team trademarks, logos, player names, numbers or likenesses, and it never presents itself as official.

Done for Phase 1 means a cleared brand name, a formed business, 8 to 10 original designs approved after samples and wash tests, and a live Shopify store selling in the US and UK. It also means Meta ads tested against real gross margin per order.

## Constraints

- **No team IP, ever.** No "Jaguars," "Jags," "NFL," logos, wordmarks, helmets, uniform designs, cat heads that resemble the team logo, player names, nicknames, numbers tied to a player (including 16), faces or likenesses. No stadium names, official slogans, or the words "official," "licensed," "authentic" or "team store."
- **Right of publicity:** Florida Statute 540.08 limits commercial use of a person's name or likeness without consent.
- **Disclaimer everywhere:** "Independent fan apparel designed in Jacksonville. Not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by the NFL or any NFL team." It shows good faith but does not make an infringing design legal.
- **Platform risk:** Shopify, Printful, Printify, Meta and payment processors all remove products, hold funds or restrict accounts after rights-holder complaints.
- **Owner time:** runs alongside an executive job search and other projects, so print-on-demand and automation come first.
- **UK VAT:** a US seller has no UK registration threshold, so VAT registration with HMRC must come before the first UK sale.

| --- | --- | --- |

Other platforms compared and set aside: WooCommerce (upkeep falls on the owner), Squarespace and Wix (weaker print-on-demand depth), Big Cartel (too limited for paid ads), and Etsy, Amazon Merch and Redbubble (aggressive automated takedowns for sports-adjacent items and little brand control).

**Target customers:** local fans in Duval, Clay, St. Johns and Nassau counties; displaced Jacksonville natives; UK fans built by the regular London games (two in 2026: Eagles at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium on 11 Oct, Texans at Wembley on 18 Oct); gift buyers; and groups such as tailgate crews, watch-party clubs and bar staff.

## Legal, IP and business formation

**Licensing:** official NFL licensing is not realistic at launch (licensees typically need an established business, guarantees and royalties; general knowledge, not verified). The plan assumes the independent fan-culture path.

**Generally safe to build on:** Duval, 904, Jacksonville, First Coast, Bold City; teal, black and gold on original art; local landmarks, the river, bridges and beaches; tailgate and game-day culture in general terms.

**Check first:** "DUUUVAL" (three Us) was filed by the team in 2018 and later appeared to be dropped, status unconfirmed. Any fan slogan the brand did not invent, such as "Sacksonville," gets a USPTO search.

**Own IP:** every designer signs a work-for-hire and copyright assignment. The brand name gets a federal trademark once cleared. The U.S. Copyright Office does not register purely AI-generated material, so mostly-AI artwork may not be protectable.

## Design

| Route | Best for | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Owner in Canva, Kittl or Adobe Express | Text-led designs, quick tests | Fine for tests; check template merch licences |
| Freelance designer (Upwork, Fiverr Pro, Dribbble, Behance) | Retail-quality illustrated art | **Recommended**, with an IP assignment |
| Local Jacksonville designer or art student | Authentic local style | Strong story; availability varies |
| 99designs contest | Logo concepts | Mixed quality, originality risk |
| AI image tools | Mood boards only | Needs a human redraw; may not be copyrightable |

**Launch collection (8 to 10):** four hero tees ("Sunday in the 904," Duval skyline over the river, a tailgate design, a teal-and-black stripe or badge), two wordmark basics (DUVAL or 904), two seasonal (holiday and playoff push), and one or two humour designs that never name the team. The prototype shows ten drafts: Sunday in the 904, Skyline Over the River, Arrive Early Leave Loud, Bold City Stripe Badge, Bridge Traffic Is My Cardio, Sweater Weather (68°F), DUVAL Wordmark Hoodie, 904 Outline Hoodie, 904 Patch Hat and DUVAL Dad Hat.

**Workflow:** brief → sketch round → IP review against the rules above → final vector art plus a 300 DPI transparent PNG at print size → mockups on each blank colour → test-print sample.

**File specs:** DTG/DTF needs a transparent 300 DPI PNG at actual size (standard front print about 12 × 16 in). Screen print needs vector art, limited spot colours, Pantone references and an underbase plan for dark garments. Embroidery needs simple shapes, no tiny text and a digitised stitch file.

| Heavyweight tee | Comfort Colors 1717, Gildan Heavy Cotton 5000 |
| Women's tee | Bella+Canvas 6004 or 6400, Next Level 1510 |
| Hoodie | Independent Trading SS4500, Gildan 18500, Bella+Canvas 3719 |
| Crewneck | Gildan 18000, Comfort Colors 1566 |
| Hat | Yupoong 6245CM dad hat, Richardson 112 trucker |
| Youth tee | Bella+Canvas 3001Y, Gildan 5000B |
| Add-ons | Koozies, tumblers, stickers, flags, totes |

Each design launches on 2 to 3 colours (black, a dark heather, and one teal or white), because every added colour multiplies bulk inventory later.

| Method | Best for | Minimum | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Screen print | Proven designs in volume | ~12 to 48 per design | Cheapest and most durable at volume; setup per colour |
| DTG | Print-on-demand, full colour | 1 | Soft; duller on dark garments |
| DTF | Full colour on any fabric | 1 | Vivid on darks; plastic feel on big solid areas |
| Embroidery | Hats, chest logos | 1 to ~12 | Premium; no fine detail |
| Sublimation | All-over prints, drinkware | 1 | Polyester and light colours only |
| Heat-transfer vinyl | Names, one-offs | 1 | Not a retail look |

**Production model:** a hybrid is recommended. Print-on-demand carries the full online catalogue, and bulk screen print or pressed transfers cover best-sellers and events. Printful (own facilities, strong branding options) and one Printify US provider each print the same design on the same blank, and the physical samples decide. Gelato is noted for later international reach.

**Jacksonville contract printer checklist (Phase 2):** quotes from three shops on the same job, setup fees, minimums, turnaround and price breaks, blind shipping and custom neck labels, a misprint reprint policy, dark-garment samples, and confirmation they'll print unlicensed fan designs at all.

**QC on every new design or vendor:** physical sample, five-cycle wash test, and photos of the samples for product and ad use.

## Inventory and logistics

- **Phase 1:** no inventory. One sample per design, plus one of each blank in the owner's size.
- **When to go bulk:** steady weekly sales for several weeks, a confirmed event or wholesale order, or pre-orders that cover the minimum.
- **Starting size curve (rule of thumb, not customer data):** S 8%, M 22%, L 30%, XL 24%, 2XL 12%, 3XL 4%. On a 72-piece run that's 6, 16, 22, 17, 8 and 3. Replace it with Shopify's sales-by-size report once there are a few months of orders.
- **Approaches:** limited drops, 7 to 10 day pre-orders, core restock with a reorder point, and print-on-demand as the backstop for rare sizes.
- **Reorder point** = (average weekly sales × printer turnaround in weeks) + safety stock.
- **Storage:** labelled bins in a climate-controlled room, never a Florida garage.
- **Shipping:** USPS Ground Advantage via Shopify Shipping or Pirate Ship. Thermal printer, scale and poly mailers. A free-shipping threshold just above one tee encourages a second item.
- **Packaging:** thank-you card with care guide and a next-order code, plus a sticker in every order.
- **Returns:** free replacement for print-on-demand misprints with a photo; 30-day exchanges on unworn bulk stock; flat-measurement size charts for every blank.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

A single UK tee leaves about $14.66 (45%) after VAT, Printful UK shipping and international card fees. That assumes Printful's UK production cost matches the US cost. Bulk screen print at 72 pieces only beats print-on-demand for in-person sales ($14.97 vs $12.73 online, where self-paid postage eats the savings). The proposal says to move online sales to bulk only at runs of about 250 or more.

<Callout type="lesson" title="What paid ads can afford">
Break-even cost per purchase equals gross margin per order. Published 2025 Meta medians are about $38.19 per purchase for e-commerce and $29.99 for lifestyle brands. Cold ads would lose money on single-tee orders at those rates. So the plan leans on bundles and hoodies, retargeting and email, organic group reach and a tight local audience. Cold ads run only as a capped test judged on real cost per purchase. These are industry benchmarks, not predictions for this brand.
</Callout>

## Lessons learned

- Fan apparel lives or dies on IP discipline. The guardrails come before any design work, not after a takedown.
- At benchmark ad costs a single tee can't pay for a cold click. Order value (bundles, hoodies) and owned channels (email, groups) carry the model.
- Bulk printing only beats print-on-demand online at much bigger runs than expected, because self-paid postage eats the savings.
