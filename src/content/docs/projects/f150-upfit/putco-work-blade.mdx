## Goal

Rear amber warning lighting across the full width of the truck, plus a steady white work light for loading while stopped. Done means the bar is stuck to the bumper, powered from J1-5, the remote works from the cab and at the tailgate, and the draws are recorded.

## Constraints

- **No drilling.** The bar sticks on with 3M tape on Putco's plastic brackets.
- **Outside the truck.** It mounts low, along the top of the rear bumper under the closed tailgate. The BooYu stays in the rear window.
- **White only when stopped.** Remote button A is white override for loading or working behind the truck.
- **Positive switching.** J1-5 can switch positive or ground, set by which fuse slot its fuse sits in (guide 14580G p.8). It must be in the positive-switching slot.
- **Draw not published.** Neither Putco document gives the bar's draw or the remote box's standby draw.

## How the CanTrol and the remote share the bar

Putco's remote wiring (93020, p.7) puts remote blue on bar blue (A = power on/off) and remote yellow on bar orange (B = change pattern). When a cab switch also powers the bar, Putco says to move remote blue to the bar's white wire, so A becomes white override. That's this build: the CanTrol is the cab switch on blue, so the remote never feeds the same wire as a CanTrol output.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| CanTrol slide 2 and 3 | Powers the bar (blue) on its amber-only pattern |
| Remote A | Latches white override on and off: steady white to the rear while stopped. Press A first to wake the remote; it lights the fob's blue LED |
| Remote B | Steps to the next pattern (a 250 ms pulse to ground on orange), so orange doesn't need to be tapped by hand |
| Remote C and D | Not used on the Work Blade. Their wires are capped |

**Routing (general knowledge):** from the amplifier under the rear seat and the accessory fuse block, out through an existing body grommet in the rear cab floor (no new holes), along the frame rail in loom, clear of the exhaust and the spare tire and its cable, then up behind the bumper. Mount the remote box on the frame or a bumper bracket near the bar, leads pointing down, out of direct spray, with zip ties or its two mounting holes. The truck's steel can shorten the 50 ft range, so test the fob before fixing the box. If range is poor, move the box higher or inside the bed rail.

## Mounting

**Where (general knowledge):** along the top of the rear bumper, under the closed tailgate, as in Putco's photo. On the bumper the wires don't flex across the tailgate hinge. Before pressing it on, check the tailgate opens and closes clear, and that the bar doesn't cover the spare tire crank access, backup camera, plate lamps or hitch. The plastic brackets can space the bar off anything in the way.

**How (Putco manual):** surface clean, dry and 60°F (15°C) or warmer. Tape on the back of the plastic brackets, prepped with the promoter. Isopropyl alcohol (90% or stronger) on the truck surface. Clip one bracket near each end of the bar, then stick it in place. The tape is very hard to remove, so set the position before pressing it on.

Tools from Putco's list: 90% or stronger isopropyl alcohol, a clean towel, pliers and electrical tape.

## Bench test on 12V before mounting

1. SUPERNIGHT 12V supply. 10A fuse in a Nilight in-line holder on the bar's blue lead, 5A on the remote's red lead. Both blacks to supply −. Bar red capped.
2. Remote standby: meter in series on the remote's red, nothing pressed. Record the draw, since it runs on constant power.
3. Bar draw: meter in series on blue, blue to +. Record the reading.
4. Remote: remote blue to bar white, remote yellow to bar orange. Press A (fob LED lights), then B to step patterns. Stop on an amber-only pattern (amber/amber on Putco's chart) and record which one.
5. White override: with blue on, press A for white. Then with blue off, press A again. Record whether white needs blue, and its draw.
6. Power off and on: confirm the bar keeps the chosen pattern.
7. Write the draws and the pattern on this page.

<Callout type="open">
The remote is on constant power. Ford's guideline for total key-off draw is 28 mA or less (see the [controller page](/projects/f150-upfit/cantrol-controller/)). The remote's standby draw needs measuring against that before it's left connected full time.
</Callout>

## Five-modes option

In the [five-modes plan](/projects/f150-upfit/five-modes-plan/) (decision D), the bar's white wire could move to a CanTrol LED output so steady white and white flash are one press. The remote's A button and the CanTrol can't both feed the white wire without isolation, so either A goes unused, or two 3A diodes let both work (general knowledge).
