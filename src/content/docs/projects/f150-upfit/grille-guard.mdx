## Goal

Mount the whole front warning package on the grille guard, so the bumper and factory grille are never cut or drilled, and so the package can come off with the guard. Done means the guard is refinished, every product is mounted with the final hardware below, a shop has bolted the guard to the truck, and every lead is stored or wired.

## Constraints

- 2017 F-150 Lariat SuperCrew. The guard is Steelcraft's 2015 to 2017 fitment (confirmed 22 October 2026).
- The bumper stays on. No changes to the bumper or factory grille.
- No drilling, welding or bending of the guard. Every mount uses existing punch plate holes, the products' own brackets, or ties around the tube.
- Every ground goes to the side of a steel frame rail or the Ford Q-231 ground point, because the body is aluminum. Never the battery negative post and never "chassis near the guard." Whether the guard's brackets make a good ground path is unknown, so they aren't relied on.
- Everything is mounted to the guard and checked on the ground first. Then a shop bolts the guard on, because the plastic splash shield has to be trimmed at the tow hook brackets.

## Measuring the lower tube

The first plan, read from photos, put the lower tube at about 1-3/4 in with a 1/16 to 1/8 in gap. On 7 October I measured it with a tape: 1.91 in OD (6 in around) and under 1/16 in between the tube and the punch plate everywhere. That gap is far too tight for any tube clamp, which is what drove the final light bar mount below.

## Light bar mount (final, 7 Oct)

The light hangs under the lower tube on its own included adjustable L-brackets, bolted to the light's rear slide-in channel about 6 in either side of center.

1. **Short leg** bolts to the light's rear slot, with blue thread locker (Loctite 242) on the bolts.
2. **Long slotted leg** lies on top of the light, between the light and the tube, with the curve turned inward so the tips stay hidden.
3. **Pad:** a black rubber anti-vibration pad, about 2 in square, sits under each bracket leg against the tube.
4. **Ties:** two JOUGU black epoxy-coated 304 stainless ties per bracket (four total) wrap the tube and pass through the bracket slot, buckles turned to the back.
5. **Tension and trim:** the ZOOSUNX tie tool snugs each tie without crushing the pad and cuts the tail flush at the buckle. Pliers and diagonal cutters work too. Touch up the cut ends with the satin black from the touch-up.
6. **Checks:** the light clears the license plate, and the ties are re-checked after the first few drives.

From the front, only the light and four thin black ties show. The brackets and pads sit hidden between the light and the tube. Mock up first with tape in place of the ties, because steel ties can't be reused. On 10 October a JOUGU tie passed through the gap behind the lower tube with plenty of room.

## Speaker mount (final, 7 Oct)

On 6 October I decided to skip the UBL L-bracket: with its flange bolted flat to the plate, the speaker would aim up or down. Each speaker bolts straight to the punch plate on its own center bolt.

- **Bolt:** the speaker's own center bolt, 1/4"-28 (SAE fine), about 1-1/2 in long, per Tyler at UBL on 7 October. The heads are stamped 8.8, a metric-style marking, so spin a nut onto one speaker bolt at the store before buying the set.
- **In front of the plate:** a stainless 1/4 in flat washer and a ready-made 1/4 in rubber or neoprene washer.
- **Behind the plate:** a stainless fender washer, 1/4 in x 1-1/4 in OD, and a stainless 1/4"-28 nylon-insert lock nut, with blue thread locker.
- **Length check:** at least two threads must show past the nut. If not, use a longer 1/4"-28 bolt.
- **No cutting or drilling.** The bolt goes through an existing punch plate hole. Confirm the speaker fits its punch plate bay before tightening.
- **Anti-rotation (suggestion, not yet tested):** one center bolt can let a speaker twist on a vibrating guard. The rubber washer and thread locker resist it. Re-check after the first drives, and if it still turns, add a second fastener or a tab that bears on a mesh hole.

Speaker photos, wiring and the sticker covers are on the [siren speakers](/projects/f150-upfit/siren-speakers/) page.

## Surface light mount (final, 7 Oct)

- **Screws:** #6-32 x 3/4 in black stainless machine screws, two per light. Tyler at UBL said on 7 October that #6-32 fits the light's holes best. If one is loose at the mock-up, step up to #8-32.
- **Why not the box screws:** the screws supplied with the lights are pan-head self-tappers about 1/2 in long (estimated from my photo, 10 Oct), meant for 1/16 in pilot holes. They fit the housing snugly but need solid metal to bite into and take no nut, so they can't hold through the punch plate holes.
- **Washers:** black stainless #8 fender washers, 1-1/4 in across, behind the plate. The punch holes are much bigger than the screw, so the washer must be about 1/4 in wider than the hole all round to span it. Measure one hole first, and go to 1-1/2 in washers if the hole is over 1 in. No adhesive tape.
- **Nuts:** stainless #6-32 nylon-insert lock nuts behind the washers.
- **Position:** the leads exit the center of the light's back, so each light sits centered over a punch plate hole and its leads run straight back through it.

<Callout type="open">
The project document's diagram labels the U-Lite washer "#6 stainless fender washer", while the hardware table says black stainless #8 fender washers 1-1/4 in across. The table is the later, more detailed entry, so it's used here. Either works as long as the washer spans the punch hole.
</Callout>

## Storing the guard wires until wiring day (7 Oct)

The products go on the guard before anything is wired to the truck. Until wiring day, every lead is secured and hidden like this.

1. **Along the guard:** run the speaker and light bar leads along the back of the guard tubes and the punch plate inside 3/8 in black split loom, held with black UV-rated 8 in zip ties, buckles to the back, so nothing shows from the front.
2. **Into the engine bay:** pass the loom through the grille near the guard's center mount, with a drip loop just before it goes in so water runs off instead of following the wire.
3. **Extra length:** coil it and tie it to the radiator support, clear of the fan, belts, hood latch and anything hot.
4. **Lead ends:** a dab of dielectric grease, then black electrical tape or a heat-shrink end cap.
5. **Labels:** label each lead with what it is.
6. **U-Lites:** the 6 in leads stay tucked behind the punch plate with taped ends until they're extended.

## Touch-up (done 23 Oct)

I refinished the used guard myself on 23 October 2026, with the guard off the truck so the back sides and bracket faces could be reached. The paint needed a 48 hour cure, so the guard was ready to mount from about 25 October.

Damage in my photos fell into three groups: surface scuffs on the tubes, chips to bare metal around screw holes, and real rust on the lower bracket tab and the plate edge.

### Method used

Spray outdoors, upwind, on a dry day between 50 and 90°F, with the next two days dry as well.

1. **Day 1, prep (about 2 hours):** mask; sand the rust down to bare metal with the 80-grit sponge; feather the chip edges with the 120-grit sponge; scuff the whole lower tube and both tabs with the 220-grit sponge; wipe down with 90% isopropyl alcohol.
2. **Day 1, paint (about 1.5 hours plus waiting):** two or three light coats of self-etching primer on the bare metal spots only. Then paint the flat end tab with the satin black as a sheen test, wait an hour and compare it with the original finish in daylight. If it matches, three light coats on the rest. Follow the recoat times on both cans.
3. **48 hours later:** mount the guard, once the paint has hardened.

The self-etching primer is dark green, so the repair looks patchy until the satin black goes on. Self-etching primer bonds to bare steel but doesn't fill, so the chip edges get feathered smooth before priming.

## Grille guard install

A shop mounts the guard, because the plastic splash shield needs trimming to reach the tow hook bracket area (decided 13 Oct). The one quote so far is 2 hours at $150 an hour, $300, adjusted down if it takes less. The wiring stays my own job.

The steps below follow the [AmericanTrucks install video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_qu9wyKAgcc) for order, tools and tightening sequence. Hardware sizes and the parts list come from Steelcraft's own install sheet. Where the two differ, both are noted.

### Before starting

- **It's heavy.** The video rates it 2 of 3 on difficulty because of the weight and uses two helpers to lift it.
- **The bumper stays on.** Steelcraft's sheet removes the bumper only to reach the top bracket bolt, which the video reaches from underneath.
- **Tow hooks** still work afterward. The bracket goes between the tow hook and the frame, and the hook bolts back on over it.
- **Front camera:** a grille camera's view would be blocked. Steelcraft says to remove the guard's center screen in that case.
- **With the UBL gear already mounted:** lift only by the guard's frame, never by the light bar or speakers. Coil and tape the wires to the guard first, and check that the light bar clears the bumper before final tightening.

### Tools

| --- | --- |
| 3/8" drive impact or ratchet and extension | Running bolts in |
| 18 mm socket | Frame bracket bolts (M12) |
| 16 mm socket + 17 mm wrench | Guard to lower bracket bolts (M10) |
| 13 mm socket + 13 mm wrench | Top bracket bolts (M8) |
| Torque wrench (borrowable from parts stores) | Final tightening |

The three top bracket designs look alike. Sort them first and set the Short pair aside.

### Steps

1. **Lower frame brackets.** Remove the tow hooks. Slide a nut plate inside the end of the frame rail and line it up with the holes. Start two M12 x 35 mm bolts (lock washer and flat washer each) up through the bracket and frame into the nut plate. Start only, don't tighten. Repeat on the other side.
2. **Top brackets.** Behind the bumper, remove the bolt at the top center of the bumper bracket. Slide the Short top bracket in. From underneath, fit the M8 x 50 mm bolt with lock and flat washers. Set the bracket's plate straight up and down and tighten this bolt fully (Steelcraft). Repeat.
3. **Hang the guard.** With helpers holding it against the outside of the lower brackets, fit the four M10 bolts from the outside in, washer and nylon lock nut inside. Snug only.
4. **Top mounts.** Bolt each top bracket to the guard with M8 x 30 mm, outside in, washer and nylon lock nut. Snug only.
5. **Center, set the angle, tighten in this order:** center the guard side to side; tilt it to the angle wanted and tighten the lower guard bolts (16/17 mm); then the top bracket bolts (13 mm); then the frame bolts last (18 mm).
6. **Finish.** Reinstall the tow hooks over the brackets with their factory bolts. Re-check all hardware after the first 100 to 200 miles, then periodically (Steelcraft).
7. **Touch up** any marks from the install with the satin black.

## Wiring to the cab

- 16 AWG speaker wire and the light wiring in split loom through the grille to the module under the driver-side rear seat.
- Rubber grommets at every metal pass-through, using a factory firewall grommet where possible: passenger side behind the glove box, or likely driver side behind the pedals.
- Both speakers in parallel with matched polarity: CanTrol orange to each speaker's white, brown to each speaker's black.
- Deutsch DT unplug points at the guard for the speakers, light bar and grille lights, so the guard can come off.
- Dedicated ground wires to a frame rail side, not the guard.
- The guard wiring stays coiled in loom on the guard until wiring day.

The full circuit list is on the [power and wiring](/projects/f150-upfit/power-and-wiring/) page.

## Scope of work

From the proposal, updated to the final decisions:

1. Mount two siren speakers to the punch plate on their own center bolts with washers, rubber washers and nyloc nuts.
2. Mount the flood light bar under the lower horizontal tube on its own L-brackets, tied to the tube with stainless ties.
3. Mount two surface lights in the upper corners of the punch plate with #6-32 screws, fender washers and nyloc nuts.
4. Run 16 AWG speaker wire and light wiring in split loom through the grille to the CanTrol, with grommets at every metal pass-through.
5. Wire both speakers in parallel with matched polarity.
6. Touch up the tie cut ends and any marks with rust-preventive paint.
7. Program the CanTrol outputs and function-test the siren tones and all lights.

## Safety notes

- Lift the guard by its frame only.
- No drilling on the guard. If a hole ever has to be enlarged, paint it.
- Spray outdoors and upwind. An N95 handles sanding dust but doesn't filter paint fumes. Wear nitrile gloves and safety glasses.
- Third-party speakers work with Whelen controllers but don't carry Whelen's SAE, California Title 13 or NFPA certification for the system.
