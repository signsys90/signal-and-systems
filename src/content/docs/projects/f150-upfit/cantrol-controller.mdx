## Goal

Run every warning light, the siren and the PA from one controller and one control head, with each light keeping its own flash patterns, and with nothing tapped into the truck's body control module except the Speed Turtle EFM 3.0.

### Module family (confirmed from Whelen's price list)

| Model | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| CANWCT2 | Standard amplifier and control module: 32 LED outputs (2.5A), 3 low-current outputs (0.25A), 8 programmable 10A outputs, traffic advisor driver |
| CANWCT2A | CANWCT2 plus an 8-input expansion module |
| CANWCT3 | CANWCT2 plus the CANport OBD-II interface module. **This is the only one that can read the brake signal over OBD-II** |

Control heads offered with the module: CANCTL4 (6 buttons), CANCTL5 (hand-held), CANCTL6 (8 buttons plus a rotary) and CANCTL7 (3-section head, 21 push-buttons, 4-position slide, mic with extension). The head's January 2020 date lines up with guide revision 14580G, so that guide's pinouts are the right reference for this unit (inferred). The seller called it "Gen 3", which is not a Whelen generation name in any document I found. It may simply mean CANWCT3 (inferred).

<Callout type="lesson">
End of life matters for a used controller. Spares and repairs now come from the used market, so the head, head cable and mic are the parts to protect. They are the hardest to replace.
</Callout>

## Pinout reference

Confirmed from guide 14580G pages 3 to 10 unless marked.

### Power and siren

| Connector | Pin or lead | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main power, 4-pin | Pins 1 and 2, black | Ground |
| Main power, 4-pin | Pins 3 and 4, red | +12V in, each fused 40A at the battery. 80A max input |
| Speaker | Pin 1 / orange lead | Speaker high (+) |
| Speaker | Pin 3 / brown lead | Speaker low (−) |

<Callout type="unverified" title="The guide disagrees with itself">
Page 7 and the J4 worksheet give one set of colors for LED outputs 11, 12, 15 and 16. Page 10 gives another, which would make LED 3 and LED 11 both orange and LED 4 and LED 12 both yellow. This build uses LED 11 (light bar left arrow) and LED 12 (Z-6 heads), so those two leads get identified by pin number and a meter, never by color.

LED outputs 17 to 32 are on J2 (J2-1 brown through J2-16 white/gray, same color order). The LED outputs are protected in groups by 20A fuses on the module. All outputs together are limited to **40A**.

## Power, grounds and ignition on a 2015+ F-150

### Main power

Two red leads, each fused 40A within 2 ft of battery positive, on 8 AWG copper. Whelen's gauge chart allows 8 AWG to 19.5 ft at 40A, and the runs are about 12 ft. 10 AWG would sit right at its 12.5 ft limit, so 8 AWG it is. The fuses go in last, after every circuit is wired (guide p.5).

### Grounds: the two rules that override the manuals

<Callout type="safety" title="Never the battery negative post">
Ford SVE Bulletin Q-231 says the 2015+ F-150 measures battery charge with a Hall-effect sensor on the negative battery cable. All negative current has to pass through it, and bypassing it "can cause a charging system malfunction and shortened battery life." Ford says to use the ground point in the bulletin (M6 bolt, 7 mm eyelet, 9 Nm) and not the negative post. The UBL manuals say to ground at the battery post. On this truck, Ford's rule wins.
</Callout>

<Callout type="safety" title="The body is aluminum">
Ford's body builder guidance says not to add grounds to aluminum sheet metal and to add ground cables only to the sides of frame rails. Every added ground in this build (lights on the grille guard, the rear plate heads, the Putco bar, the rocker bars) lands on the side of a steel frame rail or the Q-231 point. Never the cab, bed, fenders, tailgate or radiator support.
</Callout>

- **Main grounds:** both 8 AWG blacks to the Q-231 ground point, same length as before, no added cost.
- **Front devices:** a dedicated ground wire to a frame rail side. Whether the grille guard brackets make a good ground path is unknown, so they are not relied on.
- **Rear devices:** one shared ring terminal on the side of a steel frame rail near the bumper, bare metal, star washer, sealed afterward.
- **Module case:** Whelen requires the case to be mounted on or grounded to the chassis. Under the rear seat the floor is aluminum, so the case gets a short ground lead to the CanTrol's own ground run and sits on an isolator, with stainless or coated screws (general knowledge).

### Ignition sense (J17-7)

J17-7 has to see +12V only with the key on. The plan uses an add-a-circuit fuse tap. Ford's body builder guidance says not to tap the interior fuse panel or the under-hood box. The sense wire draws almost nothing, so the electrical risk is low, but it is against Ford's written guidance. The choice on record is to keep the fuse tap, picking a slot by meter (live in RUN, dead in OFF, never an airbag, engine or brake circuit), unless Ford's Body Builders Advisory Service names a better source. Fuse numbers are never taken from forums.

### Shutdown and key-off draw

Whelen Command sets a shutdown delay from Instant up to 4 hours. Ford's guideline for total key-off draw is 28 mA or less. The CanTrol idles at about 100 microamps with the key off. The Putco remote relay box is on constant 12V and its standby draw is unknown, so it gets measured on the bench.

### Inputs left unconnected

| Input | Why |
| --- | --- |
| J17-5 backlight dimmer | Whelen's default is a marker-lamp splice, which is a BCM circuit on this truck. Brightness is set in Command instead |
| J17-1 horn ring, J17-2 Park sense | No horn or BCM tap. If the module is a CANWCT3, Park can come from CANport |
| J17-8 / J17-16 radio rebroadcast | Not used. Whelen says it does not work with amplified remote speakers |

The amateur radio stays on its own battery feed and its own ground. Whelen's guide says never to power warning equipment from the same circuit, or share a ground, with radio equipment (guide p.1). See the [Mobile Ham Radio Install](/projects/mobile-ham-radio/).

## Siren and speakers

- **Wiring (confirmed):** orange to each speaker's white, brown to each speaker's black. The second speaker lands on the same two wires. Matched polarity on both, because out-of-phase speakers cancel each other in front of the truck (general knowledge).
- **Load:** 5.5 Ω minimum. Two 11 Ω UBL 100W speakers in parallel make exactly 5.5 Ω. Never a third speaker. Whelen's brochure says the CanTrol supports "one or two 100 watt speakers."
- **The 11 Ω figure** comes from the speaker listing, not a UBL document I could open. It gets read off the speaker label before wiring. A meter reads DC resistance, so 7 to 9 Ω on an 11 Ω speaker is normal (general knowledge). An 8 Ω rating would put two speakers below Whelen's minimum.
- **Wire:** 16 AWG twisted pair to the guard, about 0.12 Ω round trip over 30 ft, roughly 2% of the load (inferred). Kept apart from the head cable and the CANport cable.
- **Expected draw:** Whelen does not publish the CanTrol's siren draw. Its 295HFSA6 amp is labeled 8A at 100W and 16A at 200W, so the CanTrol should land in that range (inferred).

## Programming rules for Whelen Command

1. Start a **new** configuration. Command cannot extract a legacy CanTrol WC configuration (release notes v1.0.7.0), so the previous owner's program can't be pulled. That is fine, because a clean build is the goal anyway.
2. Every output that feeds another product's power or control wire gets **Action = Turn On, steady, Low Power disabled.** In the current map that is J1-1 to J1-6 and LED 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 9, 10, 11, 12 and 13. Each light makes its own pattern. A Whelen flash pattern or Low Power on one of those outputs would switch the device on and off or dim its input, so it would misbehave or keep restarting (inferred, bench test each).
3. Momentary jobs, like the light bar's pattern wire on LED 9, use Press On / Release Off.
4. Latched jobs (SCENE, arrows, blank button 1) use Press On / Press Off.
5. Save State stays off for warning lights, so nothing comes back on by itself at the next start (general knowledge).
6. Priorities decide which button wins when two are on. Arrows go above slide positions 2 and 3.
7. Command's "cruise" workaround (Turn On with Low Power at 1%) is never used on an output that feeds a device input.
8. Save the configuration file and keep a backup with the project files.

## CANport and brake pop

The rear Z-6 heads go steady red when the brake is pressed. That signal comes from the truck's data bus through Whelen's CANport, not from a brake-light tap.

| Requirement | Status |
| --- | --- |
| A CANWCT3 module (CANport built in) | Not confirmed. Read the label |
| Whelen CC5K1 Ford installation kit | Bought, $52.98 (21 Oct); arrived 26 Oct, in hand. Whelen lists it for 2016 to 2018 F-150 |
| Command lists a Brake signal for the 2017 F-150 | Confirmed 31 Oct 2026: Command's Configure CANport list for "F150 2017" shows 60 signals, including Brake - Service, Brake - Parking and Trans - Park (my Command screenshots) |
| CAN signals programmed in Command | Eight planned; seven keyed into my Command file "-3" on 31 Oct (brake pop first pass only). Shift out of Park not programmed yet. Not bench-tested |

The full eight-input CANport plan (brake, reverse, doors, hazards, Park, parking brake, Drive), with what each one does and its programming status, is on the [CanTrol output plan](/projects/f150-upfit/cantrol-output-plan/#canport-vehicle-inputs) and in [Upfit Studio](/projects/upfit-studio/#whelen-command-canport-setup).

If the module turns out to be a CANWCT2 or CANWCT2A, there is no CANport. The fallback is no brake pop (the Z-6 heads still do amber and clear warning), or a CANWCT3 module later. A brake-light wire tap is electrically possible but is factory wiring, and it is not planned without a Ford wiring diagram.

Known total is about 7.4A plus the BooYu and Putco, well inside the 40A combined limit unless those two are unexpectedly large. The siren has its own 20A fuse, and the two 40A main feeds cover siren plus outputs.

## Routing and mounting

- Grommet and seal every hole, use waterproof splices where moisture can reach, and keep wires out of airbag deployment zones (guide p.1).
- Use existing factory firewall grommets rather than new holes.
- Keep the 8 AWG power pair, speaker pair, CANport cable and head cable apart where practical.
- Before committing to the spot under the rear seat, look for a factory subwoofer or amplifier that some SuperCrew audio packages put there (general knowledge, not confirmed for this truck). Whelen wants the module dry and cool with its vents and fans clear.
- Route the head cable through one of the two recessed pathways on the back of the head so it can't be pinched (guide p.6).

## Common failures and how to catch them

| Symptom | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Output indicator lights but the device does nothing | Open device fuse, bad ground, or the output is in a ground-switching slot (amber indicator) |
| Whole system dead | Ignition sense not live, main fuses not in, or a blown 40A. Check the POW LED |
| Charging warnings, battery dying early | A ground that bypasses the battery sensor |
| Siren weak or distorted | Load below 5.5 Ω, or speakers out of phase |
| Light turns on and off by itself | A flash pattern or Low Power on an output that feeds a device input |
| EFM flashes the factory lamps unexpectedly | Leakage voltage on an EFM input |
| Radio noise | CanTrol sharing a feed or ground with the radio |
| Configuration won't transfer | File size limit or USB driver. Reinstall drivers from Command's About window |

## Changes this research recommends to the plan

These went to the main project document as recommendations. The ground changes are already reflected on the [overview](/projects/f150-upfit/).

1. Main grounds moved from the battery negative post to the Ford Q-231 point.
2. Every other ground now says "side of a steel frame rail or the Q-231 point," never aluminum.
3. A pre-install check for the J1-5 and J1-6 fuse slots (green indicator = positive switching).
4. The CC5K1 purchase made conditional on a CANWCT3 module and a listed Brake signal.
5. The "Turn On, steady, Low Power disabled" rule added for every output that feeds a device.
6. Ford's guidance against fuse taps recorded next to the ignition-sense choice.
7. Shutdown delay and the 28 mA key-off guideline added to the programming notes.
8. Option added to feed the rocker bars from spare J1-7 and drop the relay, if they measure under about 8A.
9. J17-5 backlight, J17-1 and J17-2 left unconnected, with Park from CANport if available.
10. The ham radio separation rule written into the main plan.
11. End-of-life status and the module family added to the unit details.
12. The LED 11 / LED 12 color conflict flagged for install day.
13. The module case grounding and isolation under the rear seat.
