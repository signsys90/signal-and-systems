## Goal

One light bar on the front of the truck that does three jobs: an amber/white warning pattern, a steady white flood for scene and work lighting, and directional arrows. Every function is run from the Whelen CanTrol WC control head, with no separate switch box. Done means every wire is landed on its planned CanTrol output, each function works from its button in the truck, and the measured draw is recorded here.

## Constraints

- **Location:** under the lower horizontal tube of the Steelcraft HD Grille Guard (50-1420C), above the license plate. No drilling, welding or bending of the guard.
- **Controller:** every function comes from the CanTrol WC. The bar makes its own patterns, so each CanTrol output feeding it is set to steady on/off.
- **Grounds:** never to the battery negative post on this truck. The 2015+ F-150 measures charge with a sensor on the negative lead, and Ford says all negative current must pass through it. The body is aluminum, so no grounds on sheet metal either.
- **Airbags:** UBL warns against mounting or routing in an airbag deployment area. This bar and its wiring are entirely outside the cab, forward of the grille.

## Architecture

The bar has eight leads. Red is its power and needs its own feed. Brown, orange and white select one of three modes, blue and green run the arrows, and yellow steps the pattern and color. Each mode remembers its own pattern and color.

Priority, confirmed on the bench: Mode 3 overrides Mode 2, Mode 2 overrides Mode 1, and the arrows override all three modes.

```text
Whelen CanTrol WC (under driver-side rear seat)
  J1-6  (10A, BLU lead) ── bar in-line fuse ── RED     power            slide 2/3, SCENE, ←, →, ↔ FLASH
  J4-14 (LED 10, BLU)   ─────────────────────── BROWN   Mode 1 warning   slide 2/3
  J1-1  (10A, BRN lead) ─────────────────────── ORANGE  Mode 2 warning   slide 3
  J4-3  (LED 1, BRN)    ─────────────────────── WHITE   Mode 3 flood     SCENE (latched)
  J4-4  (LED 2, RED)    ─────────────────────── BLUE    right arrow      → and ↔ FLASH (latched)
  J4-15 (LED 11, check) ─────────────────────── GREEN   left arrow       ← and ↔ FLASH (latched)
  J4-13 (LED 9, GRN)    ─────────────────────── YELLOW  pattern/color    blank button 2 (momentary)
  Ground run ─────────────────────────────────── BLACK   to steel frame rail side (or Ford Q-231 point)
```

Red alone does nothing. The bar lights only with red plus a mode or arrow wire, which is why J1-6 is on in every state that uses the bar. The full output map is on the [CanTrol output plan](/projects/f150-upfit/cantrol-output-plan/).

No relay is used. Every bar wire lands directly on a CanTrol output, except yellow, which goes through a 1N4004 diode (1A, 400V). The plain end goes on J4-13 and the banded end toward the bar. Without the diode the bar shuts down whenever yellow is joined to J4-13 (bench, 11 and 12 Nov). Never put the yellow wire on +12V.

<Callout type="unverified" title="Unverified: CanTrol lead colors">
My CanTrol harness is aftermarket, so Whelen's lead colors do not apply to it. On 9 November 2026 I found LED Output 27 (J2-11) is White/Amber and LED Output 28 (J2-12) is Yellow/White on my harness. On 10 November I metered LED Output 9 (J4-13), which drives the yellow pattern wire, as Green. On 12 November I confirmed LED Output 11 (J4-15), which drives the green left-arrow wire, is Violet (I had recorded it as Gray on 10 November; that was wrong). Every other lead color this bar uses is unverified. Identify each lead by connector, pin number and a meter.
</Callout>

<Callout type="unverified" title="Unverified: J1-6 switching polarity">
J1 Outputs 5 to 8 can switch positive or ground, set by which fuse slot each output's fuse sits in (guide 14580G, p.8). This is a used module, so a previous owner may have left J1-6 in the ground-switching slot. Check it before connecting the bar: the module's indicator shows green for positive switching and amber for ground switching.
</Callout>

### CanTrol programming

Set in Whelen Command. Programmed in the bench files CF1 to CF4 and passed on the bench on 12 Nov 2026 (see the [controller page](/projects/f150-upfit/cantrol-controller/#command-files-on-the-bench-12-nov-2026)). LEFT, RIGHT and FLASH also turn J1-1 (Mode 2) off so the arrows sweep clean.

| Output | Action | Switch type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| J1-6, J1-1, LED 10 | Turn On, steady | Follows slide position | No flash pattern, Low Power disabled |
| LED 1 (SCENE) | Turn On, steady | Press On / Press Off | Latched |
| LED 2, LED 11 (arrows) | Turn On, steady | Press On / Press Off | ↔ FLASH turns on both |
| LED 9 (blank button 2) | Turn On, steady | Press On / Release Off | Must be momentary. If yellow is held on, the bar reads a 2 s hold and goes to steady color |

A flash pattern or Low Power on any of these outputs would switch the bar's inputs on and off, so the bar would flicker or restart its pattern.

## Build steps

1. Bench test the bar alone on a 12V supply with a 5A fuse in the holder. Red alone on the red clip, each other wire capped separately and touched to +12V one at a time. Done 2026-10-11.
2. Set each mode's pattern and color with the yellow wire. Set Mode 3 to steady white for the flood.
3. Bench test the bar on the CanTrol, wired exactly as in the table above (Gen 3 bench guide, Stage 4).
4. Bolt the included L-brackets to the bar's rear slide-in channel, about 6" either side of center, with blue thread locker on the short leg.
5. Hang the bar under the lower tube. The long slotted leg lies on top of the bar, curve turned inward, on a rubber pad. Two coated stainless cable ties per bracket go around the tube and through the bracket slot, buckles to the back. Touch up the cut ends.
6. Check that the bar clears the license plate.
7. Loom the eight leads along the guard and through the grille, with a grommet at every metal pass-through, then run them to the CanTrol module.
8. Land the ground on the side of a steel frame rail (or the Q-231 point): bare metal, star washer, sealed afterward.
9. Program the outputs in Whelen Command and save the program file.
10. Re-check the cable ties after the first few drives.

## Testing and verification

<Gallery photos={[{ src: all295, alt: 'Wiring diagram of the Color Flood bar on the Whelen 295HFSA6 bench unit: red on T11, brown T1, orange T2, white T3, blue T6, green T7, yellow T15, black to the supply negative, with a control table.', caption: 'Every feature at once on the 295HFSA6 bench unit, tested 11 Oct 2026. **Bench-tested on 2026-10-11** (on a 12V supply and a Whelen 295HFSA6 test amp, not the CanTrol):

- All eight wire colors match UBL's instructions and the label on the bar.
- Red alone does nothing. The bar needs red plus a mode or arrow wire.
- Brown, orange and white each run their own pattern. With two on, the higher mode shows.
- Blue gives the right arrow and green the left arrow, as seen facing the bar. Both together give center-out. The arrows override all three modes.
- Yellow: a tap steps to the next pattern, a quick double tap switches amber/white, and a 2 s hold gives steady color. Each mode keeps its own setting.

**Bench-tested on the CanTrol, 12 Nov 2026: passed,** with a 1N4004 diode on the yellow wire. Slide 2 ran 60 s; yellow taps, the 2 s hold and the tap back to flashing all worked; STEADY, SCENE, RIGHT, LEFT and FLASH each ran 30 s without the bar dying; it came back after a power cycle. The arrow directions passed (RIGHT ends on the passenger side). A triple tap on yellow set slide 2's color to amber/white.

**Not yet tested:**

- Whether each mode's memory survives a power-off of at least 10 seconds.
- Measured current on the red wire in Mode 2 and in the white flood (the meter read 0 on 12 Nov, so it was skipped; the wiring is sized to the 5A factory fuse).
- Anything in the truck.

## Safety notes

- Fuse every feed. The bar's in-line fuse stays in circuit, and if it is ever removed it is replaced with the same or a similar fuse, per UBL.
- Never join red to a mode wire.
- UBL's sheet says to ground to the battery negative post. On this truck Ford's rule wins: ground to a steel frame rail side or the Q-231 point, never battery negative and never aluminum sheet metal.
- Grommet every metal pass-through and loom the whole run through the grille.
- UBL notes these are high-intensity lights. Don't look straight into the bar during bench tests, and don't operate it in hazardous driving conditions.
- Check every function visually before the truck goes back into service.

## Lessons learned

- The red lead is power only. Red alone does nothing, and joining it to a mode wire defeats the mode logic, so it needs its own output.
- On my bar, a quick double tap on yellow changed color on the 295HFSA6 bench (11 Oct). On the CanTrol (12 Nov), a triple tap set the color, as UBL's sheet says.
- The yellow input misbehaves on a CanTrol LED output without a series diode: the bar shuts down. A 1N4004 fixed it.
- Because the bar handles its own priority (Mode 3 over 2 over 1, arrows over all), the CanTrol only needs simple steady outputs. No CanTrol flash patterns are needed for this bar.

## Open items

- Measure the draw on the red wire (the in-line fuse is 5A, read 12 Nov).
- Confirm the bar's IP rating with UBL.
- Confirm J1-6 is in the positive-switching fuse slot.
- Decide where the yellow wire's 1N4004 diode lives in the truck (guard end or module end), soldered and heat-shrunk over the whole diode.
- Find the ground point on the frame rail side, or match the Q-231 point to Ford's bulletin photo.
- Decide the truck terminations (crimp butt splice with adhesive heat shrink, or a sealed connector at the guard so it can come off).
- Optional: move orange (Mode 2) from J1-1 to a spare LED output to keep a 10A output free.
- The all-lights five-modes plan may re-store this bar's mode memories. It needs no new wires.
- Add the manufacturer product link.
