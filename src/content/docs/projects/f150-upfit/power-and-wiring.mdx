## Goal

One place that lists every wire in the system, with its source, destination, gauge and protection, so wiring day is a checklist and every circuit can be traced later. Done means every row is installed, labeled, photographed and marked verified.

## The two wiring rules (21 Oct)

1. **No ground ever goes on the battery negative post.** The 2015+ F-150 measures charge with a sensor on the negative cable (Ford SVE Bulletin Q-231). Use the Ford ground point in that bulletin (M6 bolt, 7 mm eyelet, 9 Nm) or the side of a steel frame rail. The body is aluminum, so no grounds on the cab, bed, fenders, tailgate or radiator support either.
2. **Amateur radio gets its own battery feed and its own ground,** never shared with the CanTrol or its accessory fuse block (Whelen guide 14580G p.1). After install, run every electronic device at once and check for interference.

## Safe practice

- Battery negative off before any work. Fuse blocks within 2 ft of the battery, fuses out until the end, then inserted one at a time.
- Every pass-through goes through an existing factory firewall grommet or a new rubber grommet, never bare sheet metal. Factory grommets with room: passenger side behind the glove box, and likely driver side behind the pedals (8 Oct).
- Drip loops at the guard. Heat shrink and dielectric grease on exposed joints. No crimp-only joints in the engine bay or grille.
- Loom every run. Cloth harness tape inside the cab so nothing rattles.
- Bench test every product on 12V before any drilling.

Use 6 AWG only if a measured run is over 15.5 ft. Use pure copper (GXL/TXL), not copper-clad aluminum.

<Callout type="open">
The ignition sense uses an add-a-circuit fuse tap. Ford's body builder guidance advises against tapping the fuse panels. The sense wire draws almost nothing, so the electrical risk is low, but it's against Ford's written guidance. The choice on record is to keep the fuse tap, picking a slot by meter (live in RUN, dead in OFF, never an airbag, engine or brake circuit). Details on the [controller page](/projects/f150-upfit/cantrol-controller/).
</Callout>

## Battery and fuse blocks

- **Battery:** Optima Yellow Top DH7, H7 group size, 880 CCA, deep-cycle AGM ($379.99, bought). Suited to running lights and siren with the engine off. Confirm the fit in the 2017 F-150 tray and the terminal orientation before install (to confirm).
- **Fuse blocks:** two Nilight 6-way ATC/ATO blocks with covers and negative bus, near the battery. One for the CanTrol group, one for accessories. Blade fuses from the Nilight bench kit.
- **Accessory feed (P3):** about 3 ft of 10 AWG red from the battery, fused at the battery to suit the wire. 10 AWG covers the 30A rocker feed plus the smaller accessory circuits.

## Load budget

The known loads come to roughly 7.4A: the visor bar under 2.4A, the Color Flood bar at 3.3A max, the U-Lites at 0.70A, and two Z-6 heads under 0.5A each (estimate, not a measurement). The BooYu, the Putco bar and the rocker bars are not yet measured, and the siren adds about 8A per 100W speaker by the 295HFSA6 reference (inferred). The CanTrol's 40A combined output limit and the 2 x 40A main feed leave room, but the real number comes from the bench.

## Amplifier mount under the rear seat (recommended, 8 Oct)

The CanTrol module goes under the driver-side rear seat. Whelen asks for a dry, cool spot with room around its vents and fans, and for the case to be screwed or grounded to the chassis. This replaced the earlier console-bin plan. It stays dry, out of airbag zones and out of sight, and keeps the console free for the head. The rear seat folds down over an amp-sized box (confirmed 10 Oct).

```text
SIDE VIEW, driver side (front of truck to the left)

 ENGINE BAY | firewall              control head (overhead console)
   BATT ── grommet ──────────── driver seat ─────────── rear seat cushion (lifted)
            2 red (via 40A fuses) + 2 black                ┌──────────────┐
            under the driver-side sill trim  ────────────► │  AMPLIFIER   │ 2–3 in air above
                                                           └──────────────┘
                                              1/8 in aluminum plate, 4 fasteners to the floor
```

1. Lift the driver-side rear seat cushion. Take out any factory under-seat storage tray and vacuum the floor.
2. Set the amplifier in place and check three things: the head cable reaches the control head, the cushion folds down and latches without touching the amp, and nothing blocks the seat latches or seat-belt buckles. Check for a factory subwoofer under the seat.
3. Cut a 1/8 in aluminum plate (or 1/4 in HDPE) about 1 in larger than the amp all round. Screw the module to the plate through its mounting holes with lock washers (#10 x 3/4 in), keeping its vents and fans clear.
4. Fasten the plate to the floor at four points. Use existing factory bolt points if any line up. Otherwise use self-tapping screws or rivnuts, but first check under the truck so you don't drill into brake lines, fuel lines or wiring. Seal each hole with silicone. Don't rely on Velcro or tape alone, because the amp must stay put in a crash.
5. Bring the two red power wires (each fused 40A at the battery) and two black grounds through the firewall grommet and back under the driver-side sill trim. Loom them and keep them off sharp edges.
6. Run the head cable up the driver-side B-pillar and over the headliner to the overhead console, and the speaker and light outputs forward to the firewall.
7. Leave 2 to 3 in of open air above the fins, fold the cushion down, and run the bench checks again before driving.
